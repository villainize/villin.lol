SPECIAL

A conversation between Mika Nakashima, who sings the opening theme song "KISS OF DEATH (Produced by HYDE)" for "Darling in the Franxx," and Haruka Tomatsu, who plays Zero Two, has come to fruition! Enjoy the "Darling in the Franxx" talk between these two meeting for the first time!
Mika Nakashima, Haruka Tomatsu, it's your first time meeting today. First, could you share your first impressions of each other?
Mika Nakashima
"So this is the person voicing 'Zero Two'. Cool, isn't it? Zero Two's voice."
Haruka Tomatsu
Thank you! I've been listening to Mika Nakashima's music since I was a student, so I'm really happy and grateful for this opportunity to have a conversation. And to be able to sing the theme song for a project I'm involved in is really amazing. When I first heard it was when the broadcast started, and I got goosebumps thinking, "This is where the world of Darling in the Franxx begins!" The combination of the anime visuals and Nakashima-san's voice expanded the world of Darling in the Franxx even more, and I was really excited.
Mika Nakashima
I'm glad. When I first heard the demo of "KISS OF DEATH," I thought, "It's HYDE-san!" (laughs). HYDE-san had sung the guide vocals, and they were really cool and had a captivating charm. Since I received it, I had to replace it with my own expression, so the recording was quite difficult. The lyrics that left an impression on me are the first-person pronoun "Boku" and the word "Darling." They are firmly based on the story of Darling in the Franxx. HYDE-san is indeed a genius.
Haruka Tomatsu
That's true for songs like "Taste you" too, there are lyrics that make you think, "This sounds just like something Zero Two would say." Listening to Nakashima-san's singing also expanded my image of playing Zero Two. The world view was so close even before the voice recording started, and it's a really wonderful song. The music video was also fantastic! Nakashima-san's expressions and body movements were very sexy, like "a real Zero Two." How did you approach the shoot?
Mika Nakashima
At the time of shooting the music video, I didn't know the full picture of the anime, but I had a desire to match the image of Zero Two. However, if I had done it as is, it would have turned into cosplay, so I tried to incorporate the atmosphere of Zero Two while keeping my own style. I tried to make the visuals look as youthful as possible. I was thinking about various balances, like "Long hair makes you look smaller," and so on.
Haruka Tomatsu
That's interesting! Having only one wing is also part of Zero Two's image.
Mika Nakashima
The director of the music video (Takashi Tsukamoto, a visual artist) understood the world view I liked, so I think he made the video while considering the balance with the anime. Since I had horns, I might have wanted to become Zero Two too (laughs).
Tomatsu-san, you also performed "Beautiful World (TV Size ver.)", included in the ending collection.
Haruka Tomatsu
It's embarrassing to talk about singing in front of Nakashima-san, but "Beautiful World" is sung as Zero Two, so it's more like acting for me. When I'm in charge of a character song, I sing while thinking, "How would this child sing?" and sometimes I change the key to match the character.
Mika Nakashima
I feel the same way. It's like becoming a different person depending on the song.
Haruka Tomatsu
Is that so?
Mika Nakashima
Yes. Although I can't calculate "Let's sing like this for this character," when the intro starts, "that person (the protagonist of the song)" naturally comes out within me. It might change every time I sing at a live concert. It also changes depending on the audience's reaction, and when Shinnosuke Kono-san (Shinnosuke Kono, a keyboardist/producer who has participated in the works and live performances of many artists including Nakashima Mika and Maaya Sakamoto) plays the piano with an atmosphere of "Go for it more!" it changes my tension all at once. That's what I'm looking forward to, even for myself.
Nakashima-san, it seems you favor Zero Two, whom Tomatsu-san plays, in "Darling in the Franxx".
Mika Nakashima
Yes. There's a part of her that I relate to a little too much. Zero Two has a cool and mysterious side, right? I'm often seen in that image too... But in reality, I'm completely different and quite absent-minded (laughs). But when I see Zero Two, there are parts where I think, "If I were in the same situation, I might do this too."
Haruka Tomatsu
That's true! Zero Two has a mysterious side, and as the story progresses, there are clear parts and mysterious parts. She's a girl with gaps. She has a dark atmosphere and an innocent side coexisting, and she becomes very fierce when she enters combat mode. I approach the voice acting with a challenging mindset. She's a character that requires a lot of emotional energy.
Mika Nakashima
I like the line "Hey, darling of mine." With just that one line, you can really understand Zero Two's state as a character. She doesn't have many lines, does she?
Haruka Tomatsu
That's right.
Mika Nakashima
But with just "Hey, darling of mine," you can feel what's behind her, her loneliness, her strength. This word gives the impression that she's "really lonely" and also has a strong image of "You're mine." I like how various emotions are mixed.
Haruka Tomatsu
It's really nice to hear that. The word "Darling" is spoken in various situations. It might give off a sweet impression just as words, but it's also used in the title, and I think it's an important word for this work.
Mika Nakashima
That's right. There's something I wanted to ask you when we met today... Can I ask you to say "Hey, darling of mine" while matching my mouth movements?
Haruka Tomatsu
Of course!
